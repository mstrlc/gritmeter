Every ride gets a grade.

ALogo

Heavy word, thin coordinates.

The wordmark pairs a tightly set black geometric grotesk with a hairline monospace descriptor, the way a map sets a place name over its grid reference. The mark is the grade scale itself: one solid bar breaking into long dashes, short dashes and dots, the way a map draws road, track, path and rough ground. It is drawn in ink, with a colour version only where it explains the data.

BType

Two families, three voices.

A heavy display face for headlines and numbers, a plain face for reading, and a light monospace for what the machine writes: the lines in a Strava description, and data values like distances, tags and grades. In the product it stays in normal case and is never used for interface labels.

CSymbology

Colour and line, never colour alone.

Each grade has a colour that warms as the ground gets rougher, and a dash pattern that breaks up as it does. On a topo map a solid casing means paved and a dashed line means track; we keep that convention, so the map still reads in greyscale and for colour-blind riders.

GRules

      
House rules.

    

    

      
Black for words
Headlines are always 800–900. Never set display type in a light weight.

      
Mono for the machine
What gritmeter writes, and data values, go in DM Mono in normal case. Never use it for interface labels.

      
Grades keep their line
A grade colour always travels with its dash pattern or its G-number.

      
One red
Rough red, the G5 colour, marks what gritmeter adds or picks out: its lines in a description, the score pin, a selected section, focus. Nothing else.

      
Show the unknown
Untagged ways stay grey and hatched, and their share is always printed.
